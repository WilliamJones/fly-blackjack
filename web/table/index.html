      How it plays

      Every round you and the fly each get a hand against the same dealer. You act with the buttons; the fly imagines each of its options, smells which one feels best, and takes it. Whoever does better against the dealer wins the round. Ten rounds a seat.

      How it learns

      The fly's brain is 1,200 Kenyon cells, 97 output neurons and 332 dopaminergic neurons from MaleCNS v1.0, wired as measured. A situation becomes a pattern over its projection neurons; winning fires reward neurons, losing fires punishment neurons, and the synapses carrying that pattern are depressed. The brain at this table began as a fly that had played 30,000 hands alone against the dealer; every hand here is one more conditioning trial, and it persists between players.
